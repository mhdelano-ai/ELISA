Summary of one working session, 17–18 November 2026. Every detection limit in this deck is a model prediction (docs/30 Revision C; calculation kit in scripts/tier_round), not a measurement. All drawings in this deck are schematic: not to scale, and not pictures of the actual instruments. The equipment in hand: a Waters ACQUITY I-Class binary pump with a fixed-loop autosampler (100 µL loop), a Gamry Reference 600+ potentiostat (one channel) and a MicruX flow cell for printed electrode strips; both versions of the cell are available (docs/26). The 3 mm working electrode is the MicruX printed strip (docs/28 §2.1).

The request

One request, and four answers that fixed the task

The request, 17 November

“I have the following tools at my disposal: acquity i-class bsm with sm-fl (fixed loop); Gamry 600+; 10uL flow cell that can hold any micrux screen printed electrode. Design an assay that uses these items for the absolute best LOD of human IgG. Interview me if you need input.”

The interview: four questions, four answers

What may the assay add to the set-up?

“Three different implementations with these categories.”

How much hands-on work is acceptable?

Unattended per sample; preparation at the bench beforehand is fine.

Do the limits of 100 µL injected and 60 min still apply?

Both still apply.

Which flow cell and which electrodes?

Both cells are available; use whichever gives the lower limit.

Carried over from the project: human IgG in buffer only; target 1 pg/mL; the detection limit is the mean blank plus three standard deviations of blanks, read through a calibration curve.

The request is quoted as typed. The four questions and answers are from the session record (docs/32 §2); the questions are shortened here; the first answer is quoted as typed, the other three are the options chosen. The categories of the first answer were: consumables only; passive in-line parts as well; anything. “Unattended per sample” means each determination runs without a manual step from injection to result; a fresh, pre-coated electrode or bed per determination is allowed, and no unattended queue is required (docs/26). The 100 µL limit is the injected volume; 60 min counts from injection to result. Project criteria: docs/00.

The task after the interview

Three tiers, by what may be added to the equipment

Tier 1 · consumables only

Capture on the electrode itself

The capture antibody sits on the gold working electrode of a printed strip, and the whole sample passes over it.

Added: strips, thiols, reagents, a restrictor and a narrow transfer line.

Tier 2 · plus passive in-line parts

A bead bed at the cell inlet

A million beads packed against a frit in a capillary whose tip ends 1 mm above the electrode.

Added: the same reagents on beads, a fritted capillary, a sleeve and a fitting.

Tier 3 · anything

The same bed in the loop of a valve

One six-port valve closes the bed while product collects, and keeps conjugate-coated walls out of the read.

Added: one valve and its control from Python.

All three are the same sequential sandwich assay with the same reagents. They differ in where the IgG is captured and in what the read can exclude.

Tier definitions from the round brief (docs/26 §2) and the summary table of docs/30 §0. Tier 1 adds consumables only; tier 2 adds passive in-line parts; tier 3 may add anything. Tier 3 as designed uses one valve; a second valve and a second bed for a transfer of the immune complex are described and computed, but are conditional on a test of the transfer chemistry (see the transfer slide). The small pictures are schematic.

The same reagents in every tier

Biotinylated anti-Fc capture fragment on streptavidin; alkaline-phosphatase detection fragment; IgG-free albumin as blocker.

Source: docs/30 §0, §2.1–2.4. All three designs are sequential sandwich assays: sample, wash, conjugate, wash, substrate, read. Capture antibody: biotinylated goat F(ab′)₂ anti-human IgG, Fcγ-specific; detection: alkaline-phosphatase goat F(ab′)₂ anti-human IgG, F(ab′)₂-specific; streptavidin beads in tiers 2 and 3; substrate hydroquinone diphosphate (0.3 mM in tier 1, 1 mM in tiers 2 and 3). The read potential of +0.30 V against the strip’s printed reference is a starting value, to be set once per strip lot on a voltammogram of enzyme-generated product. The blank is conjugate that sticks without analyte; its spread from unit to unit is part of the limit. The drawing shows the tier-1 surface; in tiers 2 and 3 the same sandwich sits on beads.

Capture. The sample passes a 3 mm gold electrode at 2.86 µL/min for 44 min. The electrode carries the capture antibody on a thiol monolayer and streptavidin; 8.2 % of the IgG is captured.

Read. Substrate flows at 200 µL/min. The current of the enzyme on the electrode is read for 60 s, between two reference windows in the same buffer without substrate.

Why fast. Product from conjugate on the upstream walls is collected at only 6 %, the electrode’s own in full.

What limits it. Conjugate on the electrode itself, then baseline drift.

Unproven. Hydroquinone oxidation through the monolayer; the gap of the cell.

Source: docs/30 §3. Cell: MicruX strip cell with its flow add-on, a wall-jet cell with a 0.8 mm inlet bore and an 8 mm O-ring; the gap is not published and 0.2 mm is assumed (2.8 to 5.6 pg/mL for 0.1 to 0.5 mm). Electrode: printed gold strip, one per determination. 20 nM conjugate for 4 min. The signal is the read window minus the mean of the two reference windows, minus the same quantity from the identical sequence run on the fresh strip before the sample. The model puts printed gold with the monolayer at 3.6 pg/mL and printed carbon with adsorbed streptavidin at 11; if the coated gold delivers 30 % of the current the limit is 7.2. Printed carbon is the fallback, and the first tier-1 experiment compares the two. Median and range are over 2000 Monte Carlo draws; the range is the 5th to 95th percentile.

Capture. The sample passes a bed of a million streptavidin beads at 3.4 µL/min for 37 min. The bed is packed by hand against a frit in a capillary; 66 % of the IgG is captured.

Read. Substrate in, flow stopped for 5 min, then the product is swept over a printed carbon electrode in 90 s. The electrode converts 62 % of it.

What limits it. Conjugate on the walls above the bed (45 % of the blank) and on the frit (21 %), more than on the beads (31 %).

Unproven. A zero-flow step in the pump method; the wetted area of the frit; the fit of the sleeve in the inlet port.

Source: docs/30 §4. Bed: 1×10⁶ streptavidin beads of 2.8 µm carrying the biotinylated capture fragment, packed against a frit in a fused-silica capillary of 0.20 mm bore; 19 nL, 0.6 mm long, 24 mm² of bead surface. The capillary passes through the inlet port in a sleeve and ends 1 mm above a printed carbon strip; a fresh strip with every bed. 5 nM conjugate for 4 min. Read: 215 µL of substrate at 80 µL/min, 1 min at 5 µL/min, stop 5 min, sweep 90 s at 5 µL/min; the signal is the charge of the trace minus the trace of the same stop and sweep recorded on the fresh bed and strip. Model limits for a frit of 1, 5 and 20 mm²: 1.7, 2.1 and 3.4 pg/mL. If the pump cannot stop, a continuous read at 1 µL/min gives 2.8 (optimistic: the drift over the hour is not in the kit). Fritted capillary part numbers and the sleeve fit are unverified.

Source: docs/30 §5. One 2-position 6-port valve between the autosampler and the cell: port 1 in, port 6 out, the bed between ports 2 and 5, ports 3 and 4 plugged. IN (1–2 and 5–6 connected): the stream passes through the bed, for sample, washes, conjugate, substrate and the read. OUT (1–6 connected): the bed is bypassed and closed at both ends, for the 3 min accumulation, with the pump at 5 µL/min through the bypass and the cell. Sample at 3.15 µL/min for 40 min; 5 nM conjugate. Only the bed, its frit and the loop of the valve (10 mm² taken; with 30 mm² the limit is 1.7) hold conjugate that counts in the read. The steady currents of the electrode and the upstream walls (about 12 and 13 pA) are removed by the baseline. The valve is driven by the project’s Python layer.

The valve closes the bed for 3 min while the cell stays in flow, so the peak sits on a flat baseline. Signal: its area.

In all three the potentiostat holds one electrode at the read potential from the pre-run to the end. The potential is never stepped: a step would charge about a thousand times the signal at the limit.

Sketches of the expected traces, not data. Source: docs/30 §3.1, §4.1, §5.1 and §1. Tier 1: reference window 30 s, substrate front 1.1 min, read 60 s, front back 1.1 min, reference window 30 s, all at one constant flow; the two reference windows are 3.7 min apart, and the same sequence on the fresh strip before the sample is subtracted. Tier 2: the baseline is the last 30 s of the stop; the trace of the same stop and sweep on the fresh bed and strip is subtracted, because the background steps up when fresh substrate arrives with the sweep. Tier 3: the baseline is the 30 s before the valve opens; no pre-run trace is subtracted. A potential step charges about 1 µC on a 3 mm printed electrode (an unverified estimate of 0.2–1 µC, docs/29 A.4), a thousand times the signal at the limit (docs/30 §1).

Two bench questions reach the whole project

hydroquinone diphosphate, the substrate

monophosphate: what the enzyme makes first

hydroquinone

one phosphate off per turnover

1 · What does the enzyme make?

Alkaline phosphatase removes one phosphate per turnover, so at picomolar enzyme the product should be the monophosphate. If that is not oxidised at the read potential, every predicted signal is 10² to 10⁵ too high.

2 · How long does hydroquinone live at pH 9.5?

The review estimated about 7 minutes (the model takes 10), not the 2 hours assumed so far. Then the free hydroquinone of the substrate lot is gone before use, and 10–21 % of the product is lost during a stop of 3 to 7 minutes.

Both are settled by half a day at the bench, without a flow system, and both apply to the project’s earlier designs as well.

Source: docs/30 §2.6; review findings C1, C5 and M2 (docs/31). Question 1: from hydroquinone diphosphate the enzyme releases the monophosphate, which has to bind again in competition with a thousandfold excess of diphosphate before hydroquinone appears; the hydroquinone-to-monophosphate ratio after the tier-2 stop is about 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻³ (derived by the chemistry reviewer). The papers behind the choice of substrate do not address the intermediate. If the monophosphate is oxidised at or near the hydroquinone potential, nothing changes. Model, if only 1 % of the turnovers give an electroactive product: 200, 13 and 20 pg/mL for tiers 1, 2 and 3. Question 2: half-lives of 111 h, 41 h and 0.84 h at pH 7, 8 and 9 extrapolate to about 7 min at pH 9.5 (a compilation; primary source not opened); the model takes 10 min (2 min to 2 h). The test: 1–10 pM conjugate in 1 mM substrate for 7 min, voltammogram against a hydroquinone standard; the mixture separated on the liquid chromatograph; 50 nM hydroquinone read over 12 min; the working substrate solution over 8 h.
