Phase 2 · three candidates

Three ways to renew the capture surface

A · Multi-run bead-transfer card

Stirred chamber: sample, beads, conjugate

Sealed readout cell

Renewal: fresh beads dosed for every run.

Trick: a magnet drags the beads into a cell that never sees conjugate solution.

Read: one electrode, liquid at rest.

Two chambers staggered; one card per working block.

B · Single-use cells

Fresh packed bed, dried conjugate

Own electrode per cell

Renewal: a new cell for every sample, 16 cells per card, changed automatically.

Trick: nothing is reused, so carry-over is near zero by construction.

Read: liquid at rest in each cell.

Highest cost per determination.

C · Permanent glass chip

0.1 µL bed, stripped with acid

Array electrode, two channels

Renewal: the same bed regenerated with glycine pH 2 after each run.

Trick: redox cycling on an interdigitated array in stopped flow.

Read: fastest result, 37.5 min.

Later replaced by C′ (see the alternatives slide).

Source: docs/12 chip candidates. A: 170 µL stirred chamber and a 2 µL readout chamber in the first version; B: 0.5 µL streptavidin bed per cell; C: 0.1 µL anti-Fc bed. All three keep the alkaline phosphatase and hydroquinone diphosphate chemistry of the first generation.
