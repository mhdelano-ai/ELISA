Phase 1 · what the surveys found

Six facts that shaped the candidates

0

precedents for the exact combination

No integrated chip found with this enzyme label and electrochemical readout at 1 pg/mL IgG in 60 min and 100 µL.

Every one

uses a fresh capture surface

All high-sensitivity commercial cartridges found renew the surface each run. Regeneration evidence stops near 50 cycles.

93 % → 20 %

beads trapped, 5 → 20 µL/min

Magnetic trapping in flow is fragile. Dragging beads by magnet into a clean zone is the better-evidenced pattern.

37× → 4×

redox-cycling gain under flow

An interdigitated electrode only pays off when the liquid stands still.

16.5 µL

in one standard chip port

More than the whole path from capture to detector. Through-hole ports hold 0.6 µL.

> 50

valve actuations shown

Published endurance for thermoplastic membrane valves. A cartridge used for a week needs about 1000.

Sources: docs/10 chip literature (checked against abstracts, not full texts) and docs/11 chip fabrication (vendor pages; some gaps marked UNVERIFIED in its §8). Trapping figures are for 4.5 µm beads in a single-magnet chamber. Redox-cycling figure is one carbon interdigitated array at 500 nL/s. The requirements survey (docs/09) added that the capture surface’s own non-specific binding dominates the detection limit, far more than the size of the accumulation volume.

Phase 2 · three candidates

Three ways to renew the capture surface

A · Multi-run bead-transfer card

Stirred chamber: sample, beads, conjugate

Sealed readout cell

Renewal: fresh beads dosed for every run.

Trick: a magnet drags the beads into a cell that never sees conjugate solution.

Read: one electrode, liquid at rest.

Two chambers staggered; one card per working block.

B · Single-use cells

Fresh packed bed, dried conjugate

Own electrode per cell

Renewal: a new cell for every sample, 16 cells per card, changed automatically.

Trick: nothing is reused, so carry-over is near zero by construction.

Read: liquid at rest in each cell.

Highest cost per determination.

C · Permanent glass chip

0.1 µL bed, stripped with acid

Array electrode, two channels

Renewal: the same bed regenerated with glycine pH 2 after each run.

Trick: redox cycling on an interdigitated array in stopped flow.

Read: fastest result, 37.5 min.

Later replaced by C′ (see the alternatives slide).

Pre-electrolysis means electrolysing the substrate fill before accumulation to remove its free hydroquinone impurity. It rests on an assumed 1 % escape and would work on the first-generation rig as well, so it is not an argument for a cartridge. Source: docs/13 Revision B §7.1. One-step incubation 28.5 min, of which 6.35 min is paused for other runs’ reads; rinses and a 23.3 mm magnet drag follow; accumulation 11 min sealed at open circuit; then a three-window potential-step read that cancels offset and linear drift. The first orange window in each run is the pre-electrolysis monitor of the cell before the beads arrive. Restoring the margin and keeping reads quiet cost about 0.04 in P. Open point from the resolution table: two movable steps sit exactly on the guard band, so the schedule has no slack, and the checking script is not in the repository.

Alternatives B and C′

Regeneration cannot reach the carry-over limit

C fails, C′ replaces it

Captured IgG removed by acid regeneration, best figure found

92 % removed
8 % left

Needed: 99.99 % removed. A fraction never elutes, so repeating the strip does not converge, and blank subtraction gains about 10× at most.

C′: keep the permanent glass chip, but pack a fresh 0.1 µL particle bed before every injection and flush it out after the read. No valves on the chip; all switching in the dock. Result in 37.5 min.

Viable only if three mock-up results pass: end-to-end carry-over ≤ 10⁻⁴, packed-bed NSB factor ≤ 2, blank CV ≤ 12 % over 20 repacks.

B stays the fallback

16 single-use cells per card in a 2 × 8 grid, indexed by one linear axis.

Dried conjugate must deliver a dose CV ≤ 5 %; at 10 % the estimate falls to P 0.13.

Switch from A to B when an A risk fails and the packed-bed NSB factor is ≤ 2 and the cell-to-cell blank CV is ≤ 10 %.

Highest cost class: one electrode per determination.

One shared test decides both: the packed-bed NSB factor, measurable in tubes without any chip.

Source: docs/14 revised after review. The 92 % is an elution recovery in a hapten system (Sun 2008), not an IgG sandwich regeneration, so it is an indication, not a measurement of our case; no source found shows 99.99 % removal. NSB = non-specific binding of the enzyme conjugate; the “factor” is the bed’s NSB relative to blocked beads. For C′, P runs from 0.43 at factor 1 to 0.06 at factor 10. After a 1000 pg/mL sample, a residual of 10⁻⁴ takes C′ from P 0.15 to 0.10.

Blockers

Valves could not close. Channels were cut into the bottom of a 6 mm body; the membrane and plungers were on top.

Electrode pads were unreachable. The die was bonded face-down, the contact pins were in the lid.

Numbers that changed

Read drift: error 0.79 nC against a 0.05 nC target.

Timing: 58.9 min, about 1 min of margin; a 16-run card took 9.5 h.

Pre-electrolysis: about 11 % escape in the tabulated layout, not 1 %.

What held up

Every model figure the reviewer re-ran matched.

17 sources checked: none contradicted, three stretched beyond what they show.

Verdict: A stays the leading concept; wait for gate G1 and the E1.2b result.

Source: docs/15 chip review. The reviewer recomputed the magnet forces, diffusion and electrolysis time constants, charging charge, timing table and carry-over paths with its own scripts, and re-ran a sample of the model estimates on a copy of the repository. Other significant findings: a read window collided with valve and pump activity of the run two places later; the magnet geometry could not gather beads from the far half of the chamber; stranded beads reached a later incubation; A’s carry-over budget was not allocated; a modelling error in the C′ carry-over table flattered C′.
