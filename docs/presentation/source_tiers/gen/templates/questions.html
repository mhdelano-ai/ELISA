Before anything is built

Two bench questions reach the whole project

{{art:product}}

1 · What does the enzyme make?

Alkaline phosphatase removes one phosphate per turnover, so at picomolar enzyme the product should be the monophosphate. If that is not oxidised at the read potential, every predicted signal is 10² to 10⁵ too high.

2 · How long does hydroquinone live at pH 9.5?

The review estimates about 7 min by extrapolation (unverified; the model takes 10), not the 2 h assumed so far. If so, the free hydroquinone of the substrate lot is gone before use, and 10–21 % of the product is lost during a stop.

Both are settled by half a day at the bench, without a flow system, and both apply to the project’s earlier designs as well.

Source: docs/30 §2.6; review findings C1, C5 and M2 (docs/31). Question 1: from hydroquinone diphosphate the enzyme releases the monophosphate, which has to bind again in competition with a thousandfold excess of diphosphate before hydroquinone appears; the hydroquinone-to-monophosphate ratio after the tier-2 stop is about 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻³ (derived by the chemistry reviewer). The papers behind the choice of substrate do not address the intermediate. If the monophosphate is oxidised at or near the hydroquinone potential, nothing changes. Model, if only 1 % of the turnovers give an electroactive product: 200, 13 and 20 pg/mL for tiers 1, 2 and 3. Question 2: half-lives of 111 h, 41 h and 0.84 h at pH 7, 8 and 9 extrapolate to about 7 min at pH 9.5 (a compilation; primary source not opened); the model takes 10 min (2 min to 2 h). The test: 1–10 pM conjugate in 1 mM substrate for 7 min, voltammogram against a hydroquinone standard; the mixture separated on the liquid chromatograph; 50 nM hydroquinone read over 12 min; the working substrate solution over 8 h.
